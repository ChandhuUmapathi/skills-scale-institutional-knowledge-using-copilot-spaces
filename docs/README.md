# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository contains the OctoAcme project management process documents used to guide project initiation, planning, execution, risk management, release, retrospectives, and role alignment. These documents provide a shared operating model for how the team defines work, manages delivery, communicates progress, and maintains quality across the full project lifecycle.

## Overview of OctoAcme project management processes

OctoAcme follows a structured lifecycle that begins with project initiation and ends with review and continuous improvement. Teams validate the business need, define measurable outcomes, identify stakeholders, and document a lightweight project charter or one-pager that captures goals, milestones, risks, and initial resource needs. Once the initiative is approved, work moves into planning, where the backlog is prioritized, scope is estimated, dependencies are mapped, and a Definition of Done is established so the team can execute with clarity and shared expectations.

The process emphasizes clear ownership and cross-functional collaboration. Product leaders define the customer problem and success metrics, Project Managers coordinate delivery, scheduling, communication, and risk tracking, and Developers and QA/Testers are responsible for implementation, validation, and quality assurance. Stakeholders provide alignment and decision-making input as needed. This separation of responsibilities helps keep work customer-focused while ensuring accountability for execution and delivery outcomes.

Communication is treated as an ongoing part of project management rather than an isolated task. The workflow includes daily or regular team check-ins, weekly alignment updates, milestone reviews, and escalation paths when blockers require additional support. Risks and dependencies are tracked in a shared register and reviewed regularly so issues can be mitigated early. For critical incidents or unexpected disruptions, standard communication templates and escalation paths support clear, timely updates across teams and stakeholders.

Quality assurance is embedded throughout delivery and release. The project process requires visible acceptance criteria, reviewable pull requests, CI validation, testing, security checks, and documented release readiness before deployment. Teams use smoke tests for key flows, protect against regressions, and validate post-deployment outcomes before declaring a release successful. After delivery, retrospectives capture what worked well, what should improve, and which action items need follow-up. This creates a continuous improvement loop that reinforces team learning and operational consistency.

## Why these docs matter

These resources provide a standard, shareable foundation for cross-functional delivery and help the team stay aligned on roles, steps, checks, and communication expectations. They support onboarding, planning, and execution by making project management habits explicit and easier to repeat across teams and projects.
